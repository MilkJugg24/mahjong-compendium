- **[1](../../../../../../REFERENCES.md#ref-1)** — NOT FOUND. Neither Sloperama's catalogue nor any Mahjong Wiki page fetched here carries an entry for Hong Kong Taiwanese or HKT. The chart's node rests on the Sloperama catalogue as its canonical source, but the label does not appear there
- **[66](../../../../../../REFERENCES.md#ref-66)** — MULTI-PARENT: describes the game as a blend (糅合) of Hong Kong mahjong, authentic Taiwanese mahjong, Japanese mahjong and MCR, with fan counts far larger than authentic Taiwanese, contrasted with traditional 13-tile Clear Chapter (清章) play. Locally it is simply called Taiwanese mahjong, 台灣牌 or 港式台牌. It has a ready declaration called ding (叮) which, unlike Japanese riichi, needs neither a concealed hand nor a stake, and pays a bonus for winning within one turn; and a carry-over stake mechanism called la (拉). The article carries a single-source reliability warning dated March 2023, and cites a Hong Kong Mahjong Association league rules PDF from 2020

**Reading it:** No longer unsupported. Chinese Wikipedia carries a full article on 港式臺灣麻雀, the 16-tile game of Hong Kong and Macau, giving 144 tiles with eight flowers, a 16-tile hand, additive and unlimited fan, a ready declaration called ding and a carry-over stake called la, and citing a Hong Kong Mahjong Association league rules document. Two cautions. The article itself carries a single-source reliability warning, so it is one account rather than a consensus. And it contradicts the tree's shape: it describes the game as a blend of Hong Kong, authentic Taiwanese, Japanese and MCR rules, where the chart makes it simply a child of Taiwanese style. Its additive scoring is also the feature the article uses to contrast it with Guangdong, which the chart places on a different branch entirely.

All sources above were fetched and read on 2026-09-18. Nothing here is second-hand: a claim that could not be read at its source is not in this table.
