- **[1](../../../../../REFERENCES.md#ref-1)** — revealed by Thierry Depaulis in a newsgroup post, 1 May 2006
- **[80](../../../../../REFERENCES.md#ref-80)** — PRIMARY SOURCE, AND IT IS A LAW. Simplified mahjong is form (b) of Macau's official casino regulation, introduced when Portaria 135/91/M replaced the 1989 rules, which had no such form. Two to four players; the wall is four double rows of 14 stacks; a set of three and a pair wins; neither chow nor pung may be claimed from a discard, which can only be taken to win, though kongs are allowed with a replacement draw
- **[83](../../../../../REFERENCES.md#ref-83)** — Portaria 135/91/M is still the regulation the Macao gaming bureau names for Mahjong, so the simplified form remains an authorised casino game rather than a lapsed one

**Reading it:** 112 tiles and a 4-tile hand winning on 5 are now confirmed from the law that created them. Sloperama, working from a 2006 newsgroup post, catalogued the game correctly: Macau's official casino regulation, Portaria 135/91/M, gives 112 tiles — the three suits and the green dragon — a hand of 4 winning on 5, no flowers, and payment to the winner only. It adds what the catalogue could not: this is not a regional style that grew up in Macau but a second, faster form written into the casino rules in 1991, where a discard may be claimed only to win and never for a chow or a pung, and where the House takes a commission on every won game. Its extreme hand size is a casino's doing, not a region's, and the regulator still names those rules today.

All sources above were fetched and read on 2026-09-19. Nothing here is second-hand: a claim that could not be read at its source is not in this table.
